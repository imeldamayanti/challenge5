# Kultara — Affinity Diagram

Source data grouped into themes, with every note tagged as a **Finding**, **Insight**, or **Pain Point**.

- 🔵 **Finding** — an observed fact straight from the interview/board.
- 🟢 **Insight** — an interpretation of *why it matters* ("so what").
- 🔴 **Pain Point** — a problem, friction, or unmet need.

---

## 1. Guide Role & Work Structure

- 🔵 Guides work freelance across 4–5 travel agents; roles vary (guiding, airport transfers, group collection).
- 🔵 Licensing is per-province via HPI — ~2 years of supervised work before an own license; this year's quota was cut and postponed.
- 🔵 Kultara is a social business, not a formal agency — no license required; it trains storytellers via Kultara Academy.
- 🟢 Guiding attracts people who value flexibility over structured office work — motivation is freedom, not just income.
- 🟢 Because Kultara sits outside formal licensing, it has freedom to design its own experience and training — room for product innovation.

## 2. Tourist Segments & Demographics

- 🔵 Kultara open trips = mostly Indonesian; private trips = highly mixed nationalities (AU, SG, HK, couples, groups, students).
- 🔵 ~20–30% of Kultara guests are repeaters (usually a different route); the rest are first-timers.
- 🔵 Teens on school trips show low interest; the trip is booked by teachers, not the students.
- 🔵 Self-booking adults arrive already interested — they researched the route beforehand.
- 🔵 Older guests avoid walking; they prefer food tours, 1–3 spots, and relaxed listening.
- 🔵 Indonesians handle heat poorly → they choose early-morning or late-afternoon routes.
- 🟢 Interest level tracks *who booked and why* far more than age or nationality — self-selected guests are pre-engaged.
- 🟢 The real engagement challenge is low-interest segments (teens, tag-along companions), not "foreign vs local."
- 🔴 School groups have low buy-in because the decision-maker (teacher) isn't the participant.

## 3. Engagement — Hands-On = Memorable

- 🔵 Engagement peaks during hands-on activities: wood carving, plowing rice fields with cattle, dancing.
- 🔵 Post-tour highlights are the active/moving moments (dancing with kids, the turtles, the museum).
- 🟢 Memory is built by *doing*, not by listening — "when something moves or they're in charge, that's most interesting."
- 🟢 Passive explanation is the weakest part of the experience; participation is the strongest.

## 4. Disengagement & Boredom

- 🔵 Boredom appears when a guest is only accompanying an interested friend.
- 🔵 Dea re-engages by asking questions / opening Q&A; if interest is lost, she lets them be.
- 🔵 Photo-taking and phone use are tolerated as long as guests stay with the group and aren't bored.
- 🟢 The guide triages attention toward the engaged and doesn't fight disengagement.
- 🔴 "Tag-along" companions get no tailored experience — the guide effectively gives up on them.

## 5. Information-Seeking Behaviour

- 🔵 Tourists pre-search on Google but only superficially; they rely on the guide for detail.
- 🔵 Foreigners ask "what is this? / what is that?" about new things while walking.
- 🔵 Indonesians ask to validate curiosity or something they heard from a friend.
- 🟢 The guide is the primary, trusted information layer; digital search is shallow and happens pre-trip.
- 🟢 Segments ask different question types — discovery (foreigners) vs validation (locals) — so content could be tailored.

## 6. Operational Friction & Pain Points

- 🔴 Heritage sites close suddenly for Balinese ceremonies → the guide must skip or divert.
- 🔴 Guests can't swap a disliked destination (it's pre-decided) — the only option is to skip.
- 🔴 The most common complaint is the route — tired, too far, too winding.
- 🔴 Open trips get complaints when bookings are low and the group feels empty; guests want it lively.
- 🔴 Detailed permit/licensing questions are beyond a guide's authority to answer.
- 🔴 Free-time gaps (guide prepping tickets/bikes) leave guests idle.
- 🟢 Idle / unguided moments are a recurring, unowned gap — the clearest product opportunity.
- 🟢 "Empty = bad" for open trips shows social atmosphere is part of perceived value, not just content.

## 7. Opportunities & Product Direction

- 🔵 Routes are tuned early via feedback (Sanur was shortened for more cycling), then fixed.
- 🔵 The interview explicitly probed for a "helper tool" to fill idle time and boost engagement.
- 🟢 Biggest leverage = self-guided content/tool for unguided free time (aligns with the team's prototype direction).
- 🟢 Since routes are now fixed, experience improvement must come from layering content/interaction, not re-routing.
